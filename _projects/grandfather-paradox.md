### Synopsis

David has had enough of living. Convinced that humanity's future holds nothing but suffering, he travels back in time to erase himself from existence by preventing his grandparents from ever meeting.

But with only minutes to spare, he's confronted by Indigo, a time traveller from 2095 who claims to be his daughter. She's come to stop him, and she's living proof that his future holds something he never expected: a family.

As the clock ticks down, father and daughter clash over whether a life filled with suffering can still be worth living. David believes he's sparing them both from a cruel existence. Indigo insists that even broken lives contain moments of joy worth fighting for.

When David finally puts his plan into action, the universe responds with a cruelly ironic twist: his attempt to prevent his grandparents' first meeting becomes the very thing that brings them together.

Trapped in a self-correcting paradox, David is forced to confront a future he never wanted, and the possibility that, despite everything, his life might have meant something after all.

With a little gallows humor and an absurdist twist, *Grandfather Paradox* is a existential, bittersweet, science ficture short about despair, the unexpected consequences of our choices, and the difference between living a perfect life and living one worth having.

### Writer's Note

In *Back To The Future*, Marty McFly accidentally prevents his parents from meeting, threatening to erase him from existence. 

I thought... what if he did it on purpose? What if he *wanted* to erase his own existence?

I've suffered from severe depression, and suicidal ideation. I know what it's like to look into the abyss and want to jump. And I have often stopped myself from doing so, not because I didn't want to die — but because I knew the pain it would cause to those I left behind. 

But a time traveler doesn't have to leave anyone behind, if they never exist. 

It's a loophole in the moral consequences of suicide.

Do we still have an obligation to others to stay and suffer? 

*Grandfather Paradox* doesn't offer any answers. It just proposes the question. 

There was a challenge I set myself with this: to make a very, very low budget time-travel movie. This film offers one location, a twist ending, and eleven pages. I think it turned out well.
