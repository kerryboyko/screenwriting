### Synopsis

Kelly O'Connell is a forty-three-year-old stand-up comedian who has spent most of her life battling depression without fully understanding why. After finally realizing she is transgender, she begins transitioning and finds herself happier than she has ever been.

Hoping to share that happiness with the people she loves most, Kelly returns home to North Carolina for Thanksgiving, determined to reconnect with her Catholic Irish-American family as her authentic self.

Kelly wants to be herself. Her family wants to keep things polite.

What follows is not outright rejection, but something more complicated: a battle between love, grief, denial, faith, politics, and the unspoken rules of civility. As Kelly struggles to preserve her relationships without sacrificing her identity, every attempt to keep the peace demands another concession.

But after decades of depression and suicidal ideation, what begins as an awkward family conversation gradually reveals itself to be a matter of survival.

<div class="project-writers-note" markdown="1">
### Writer's Note

*Polite Company* is a deeply personal work, inspired by my own experiences of coming out as transgender in middle age and navigating the consequences within my family.

It is an autobiographical *roman à clef*, and far more faithful to actual events than audiences might expect. Much of the dialogue, including some of its most uncomfortable exchanges, has been reproduced verbatim.

But *Polite Company* is about more than one family's response to a transgender daughter. It is about how power protects itself by reframing harm as a problem of tone, and how the demand to keep the peace can become a demand for silence.

As a stand-up comedian, I've spent years exploring how humour can make uncomfortable truths easier to confront. But I've also learned that humour can be used to avoid confronting those truths altogether. And, like Kelly, I've learned that there are times when humour simply stops working as a coping mechanism.

The stand-up performance that frames the film is not merely a storytelling device. Throughout the story, Kelly is interrupted, corrected, and told to keep her feelings to herself. Onstage, she finally gets to tell her story, uninterrupted.
</div>
