The crew of a tugboat recover material from a tragic wreck only to find that there are survivors. With no one else available to help, the three working-class space-haulers have to figure out how to get the survivors to safety before their life support runs out. 

## Synopsis

It's cheaper to recover scrap from wrecks than it is to launch new material into space, so there's a demand for small, tough ships, basically tugboats, to recover salvage from wrecked spaceships. These have small crews, maybe 3 people, and it's claustrophobic. They're good at their jobs but these are not heroes or scientists.

The crew of a tugboat recover material from a tragic wreck, only to find that there are _survivors_ who have managed to seal off and have life support working... barely.

They can't take them on board, not enough life support on the ship for 6 people on the tugboat. And if they try to take the entire ship, they won't get back to port in time to save the survivors. They have maybe half a day's worth of oxygen for a three-day voyage. 

So, in space, the 3 person crew have to figure out how to either get the survivors more oxygen or figure out how to get back to port _faster_. They're able to talk to an expert to talk them through the rescue via their radio, but there's a difference between talking them through the procedures and actually being there. 

Eventually, they figure out a way to do it: The crew has to spacewalk, _while the ship is being towed_, and figure out a way to jettison parts of the larger spacecraft.

Once they do figure out how to do that, they think they're home free. But there's a complication. 

They have just enough fuel to get the survivors to safety. 

But it's space. 

They _don't_ have enough fuel to decelerate and stop _once they get there._ 

Other tugboats answer the call, and when the tugboat and wreck get close, they match it's velocity, and attempt a high-speed hand-off of the wreck so that the tugboat has enough fuel to brake itself.  

The team disembarks and lands on the station to a hero's welcome. They'll enjoy it later, they just need to sleep, so they go back inside their tugboat to rest in their bunks.

### Writer's Note

With this, I've taken the central appeal of _The Martian_ and inverted it. Instead of an extraordinarily capable scientist using his expertise to survive an impossible situation, our working-class tugboat crew are confronted with an impossible situation they are completely unqualified to solve—and they solve it anyway. 

To be fair, they're extremely competent at what they do. But what they do is salvage.

They don't know how to repair a sophisticated life-support system. They don't know orbital mechanics beyond what their navigation computer handles. They aren't trained in emergency medicine.

And now six people are going to die unless they can figure out something nobody has trained them to do.

It's also a natural thriller escalation, using physics. 

The tugboat could get home faster, but it has limited fuel. 

Reducing the mass it tows allows it to accelerate more effectively and get home sooner. 

But they can't jettison the wreck - the survivors are inside it. 

They need to cut away from it. But they can't cut away too much because they'll compromise the pressurized compartment. 

Then, if they fire the engines too hard, the wreck might not hold because the structural integrity is questionable, and the fastest trajectory to help might require accelerating at a rate the damaged wreck can't withstand. 

Every solution creates a new engineering problem: a natural escalation. A classic thriller engine.
