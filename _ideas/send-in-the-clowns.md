Four comedians, backed by real lawyers, attempt to solve real societal problems by writing actual legislation and presenting it directly to politicians.

## Format

Each episode, four comedians — backed by real lawyers — attempt to turn their ideas into real legislation and pitch them directly to politicians, forcing them to respond on camera.

The show combines the irreverent energy of a comedy panel show with the investigative curiosity of a documentary to create _participatory political comedy_. The comedians don’t just mock the system. They enter it. 

Even when the proposals fail, the process sparks debate and reveals how political systems actually work. When they succeed, the show demonstrates that determined citizens can still change policy.

Each episode will contain: 

*   **Problem Drop (5 min)**The panel is given a real societal issue. Housing shortages. Phone scams. Food waste.
    
*   **Comedians Debate (10 min)**Comedians argue, joke, and propose wildly impractical (and some weirdly plausible) solutions.
    
*   **Research (10–12 min)**Field segments with experts, victims, and lobbyists.
    
*   **Legislation Workshop (10 min)**Real lawyers attempt to turn the comedians’ ideas into actual legal text.
    
*   **Pitch to Politicians (5–8 min)**The comedians present their proposal to real politicians.
    
*   **Tracking Segment (series arc)**  What happened to previous episodes’ bills?
    

The panel would combine different comedic instincts:

*   **The Absurdist** who proposes ridiculous solutions
    
*   **The Policy Nerd** who actually understands politics
    
*   **The Cynic** who distrusts everything
    
*   **The Chaos Agent** who pushes the debate off the rails

The show is guided by a host who moderates the discussion, challenges the panel’s ideas, and keeps the legislative process moving.

Inspiration from the show comes from the fact that late night comedians, talk show hosts, panel guests often have _commentary_ on the issue, but rarely step up to fix it. The exceptions are extremely noteworthy, specifically Jon Stewart’s tireless campaign for the healthcare of 9/11 first-responders.
