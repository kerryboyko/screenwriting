When the founder of a conversion therapy camp dies, a con artist swoops in and rebrands the entire operation, turning it into a secret refuge for queer teens—outsmarting the right-wing watchdogs with scams, stunts, and lies bold enough to keep the truth hidden and the kids safe.

## Synopsis

The True Directions camp (nicknamed “Fix-A-Kid Ranch” by its inmates) was supposed to be just another drop in the bucket of transphobic “correctional” institutions. But when the founder dies—mysteriously?—a fast-talking con artist swoops in and rebrands the whole operation.

On paper, it’s still a conversion therapy camp. In practice, it’s a deep-cover queer sanctuary.

It's a money-grab by a shameless liar, opportunist, and occasional coward. But the longer the grift runs, the higher the stakes—and the more the con artist must confront the question: What if you stop running and start leading?

### Comps

_Jojo Rabbit_ meets _The Ballad Of Buster Scruggs_ and _They/Them_ without the serial killer.

### Themes

The Trickster's Journey. Outwitting dimwits with power. Fighting back with wit instead of weapons. Examining survival through sabotage, deception, and the impossible math queer communities face in much of America. And a classic Western question: Can someone whose entire life has been built around running away become the person who stays and fights?

### Tagline

_The only honest thing happening at Fix-A-Kid Ranch is the fraud._

### Writer's Note

The tone is strong and the premise is potent—but sustaining the story beyond a short would require building a credible scaffolding of absurd but survivable hijinks. What’s the structure? What’s the endgame? I have questions. But I believe in the world.
