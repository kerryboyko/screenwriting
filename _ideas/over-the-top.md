## Synopsis

Extreme ridiculousness played with extreme sincerity. 100% overcommitment to the bit, 100% of the time. Pathos and bathos, inseparable, punctuated by bursts of pure parody and strangeness. That is the ethos of _Over The Top_, as a satirical workplace comedy-drama: misfits learning to turn their strangeness to their advantage. They’re risk-takers in a notoriously risk-averse industry: media. They don’t try to game the algorithm. The algorithm can’t figure out what they’re doing. Because they don’t have any idea what they’re doing either.

MORGAN PRICE sees himself as the responsible adult. He's wrong. He's a visionary who has convinced himself that his particular form of absurdity is fiscal responsibility. Morgan built an Internet content studio for comedy shorts with a collection of weirdos, brought it to a streaming platform, then sold it to that platform. Now, six months in, the acquiring corporation decides to kill it — not because it isn’t profitable, but because it’s not profitable enough. 

Rather than abandoning his employees, Morgan responds by doing the most fiscally responsible thing imaginable: spending his savings and mortgaging his house to buy it back. One problem: he has just learned what happens when someone else controls your distribution. He refuses to make that mistake twice. His perfectly reasonable solution: become his own distributor by launching a FAST channel and on-demand subscription streaming service. His desire to protect his team’s vision constantly conflicts with his responsibility to keep the business alive. Somehow, he must make the clown car scale without turning it into an SUV. 

His closest confidant is the gifted, if slightly deranged, director RILEY KNOX. Her ideas for production seem ludicrous when pitched but actually work on camera. Riley is the one who can make a papier-mâché model and a hobby drone look nearly as epic as a million-dollar CGI shop. The problem is, she also doesn’t know when to cut her losses: most of her ridiculous ideas eventually work, so naturally she has absolutely no internal mechanism for recognizing when one isn't going to.

Their crew includes SPUG, a zen-surfer comedy writer; JOCASTA, explosively enthusiastic on-air talent; NEENEE, an operations manager who weaponizes uncomfortable silence; and TREY, a camera operator willing to sacrifice anyone's dignity for the shot.

Desperately seeking traction, Morgan meets with producer DAVID HEIFFENMUELLER, who had great success with off-the-wall comedies in the 80s and 90s, but after a series of flops and the downturn in comedy after 9/11, wants to find a hit that will bring him back to his glory days as Hollywood’s hit-maker. David knows agents. Advertisers. Stars. Distributors. Studios. Journalists. Attorneys. Everyone who mattered in Hollywood thirty years ago, plus a surprising number who still do. He proposes being a silent partner in the business, though his version of “silent” is anything but. 

The combination of new media savvy with old-media cunning might work for three reasons: Morgan knows how to operate a media company. Riley knows how to make things work that shouldn’t. And David knows every trick old Hollywood ever learned about finding an audience. Whether any of those tricks still work is another question.

Over The Top is an episodic comedy with short-term bits and long-form consequences. A typical episode begins with an immediate threat to the studio's survival. (The studio has been vandalized by wild animals.) Morgan attempts the sensible business solution. (He hires pest control.) Because of some unforeseen absurdity, it makes everything worse. (The infestation turns out to be poodle ghosts.) When things look dire, the team attempts something that absolutely shouldn't work. (A poodle seance led by a veterinary clairvoyant.) It does. For now. 

By betting everything on the proposition that audiences still want things nobody would rationally commission, Over The Top constantly lives on the brink of disaster, with every victory bringing some new wrinkle. Morgan, Riley, and their team have to learn to embrace the absurdity, and hold on for dear life. 

### Comps

Combining the prestige media satire of _The Studio_, the workplace absurdity of _30 Rock_, and the anything-can-happen Dadaism of _UHF_ and the Zucker-Abrahams-Zucker films, _Over The Top_ is a story about dreamers and darers out-crazying the insanity of modern media.


### Themes

The absurdity of how Hollywood counts success. Found family. Getting great work out of talented people—especially _insane_ talented people. 

### Tagline

_If we don't know what we're doing, the algorithm couldn't possibly know either!_

### Writer's Note

This started as a love-letter project to Jay Levey's _UHF_, one of my favorite movies as a kid. 

Indeed, before I created this, I approached Mr. Levey and asked for permission to send him one-page pitch to bring _UHF_ into a prestige television series. He wrote back in a very polite letter thanking me for reaching out, but since he didn't have ownership of the IP, it wasn't really something he was interested in pursuing. 

I then essentially took the original characters I developed for that spinoff, removed any _UHF_ characters or branding, and created this idea from it. I'm rather pleased. 

And Mr. Levey, if you somehow ended up on this webpage, thank you for the kind words. It means a lot.
