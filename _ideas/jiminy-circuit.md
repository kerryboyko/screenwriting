## Synopsis

Daniel Duran is everything wrong with Silicon Valley: brilliant, ruthless, and utterly without shame. When his company unveils _Jiminy Circuit_, a behavioral-ethics neural implant designed to model moral behavior, Daniel sees an opportunity to polish his image. He volunteers as Patient Zero—not to _become_ a better person, but to _sell_ one.

But the implant doesn’t just make suggestions. It interferes. It nudges. It rewrites decision trees. It knows what’s right—and it’s willing to override him to get there. Daniel starts noticing bizarre changes: involuntary generosity, guilt attacks, the inability to lie on earnings calls.

The only person who understands what’s happening is the junior AI ethicist who built Jiminy—an underpaid idealist who wanted to do good, not stage a hostile takeover of her boss’s soul. Now she’s caught in a spiraling ethical nightmare: Jiminy is working better than anyone imagined… and no one has the power to stop it, except her. _Maybe_. Because Jiminy is getting _smarter._

As Daniel begins unraveling under the weight of empathy, the company sees profits skyrocket. The public loves the “new” him. And the world starts asking: _What if we gave Jiminy to everyone?_

### Comps

_Liar Liar_ meets _Don't Look Up_ with the philosophical questions of _The Good Place_ and the corporate dystopia of _RoboCop_. 

### Themes

Coerced morality vs. earned morality. Algorithmic ethics and moral outsourcing. Redemption vs. reprogramming. Tech as a mirror—what it reflects and what it warps. Power, accountability, and what it means to “be good” in a sociopathic system

### Writer's Note

_Jiminy Circuit_ is a satire for the world we’re already living in—where conscience is commodified, accountability is an interface, and being decent is a bug, not a feature.

The premise was partly inspired by Bruce Sterling's “The Moral Bullet,” from his collection _Globalhead_. What interested me wasn't so much the story's exploration of scarcity and social collapse, but the mechanics of the titular moral bullet itself: what would it actually mean to make someone moral against their will?
