A “time-queer” exile—born from a paradox and rejected by every timeline—flees across the multiverse, hunted by enforcers who insist she should never have existed. Her only hope: to anchor herself in one stable reality before she’s erased for good. But the longer she survives, the more the multiverse warps around her—and maybe that’s exactly what it needs.

## Synopsis

The human race was supposed to end decades ago—nuclear war, environmental collapse, the usual. But time travelers from our doomed future kept going back to fix it. Every time they succeed, someone else arrives from the next apocalypse. Survival becomes recursion. Each fix makes the timeline weirder.

Humanity has survived the apocalypse so many times that reality itself is becoming an accumulation of increasingly desperate patches. Technical debt for the universe. And sometimes, a bit of undefined behavior slips through the cracks. 

Enter Rosemund Geidroyc—a “subprime” time refugee born from a paradox timeline that shouldn’t exist. She carries a stolen temporal device. To the powers that be, she’s an error. A glitch. A walking contradiction whose very presence destabilizes reality.

But Rosemund isn’t breaking time for fun. She’s trying to survive—and prove that even an “impossible person” deserves a place in the universe.

### Comps

_Loki_ meets _Red Dwarf_ meets _The Fugitive_. 

### Themes

Exile, survival, and identity. Queerness as a temporal paradox. Erasure as violence—resistance as presence. Historical control, “normalcy” as weapon, and the horror of timelines where everything went “right.”

### Tagline
