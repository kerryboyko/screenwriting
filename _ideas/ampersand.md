When a flashy stage illusion turns deadly, a disgraced illusion designer becomes the prime suspect in a magician’s death. To clear his name, he must unravel the trick himself—with help from a silent ex-performer who may be the only one who saw the murder for what it really was: misdirection.

## Synopsis

Ampers is brilliant, bitter, and stuck designing tricks for a bombastic London magician he openly despises. One night, a signature illusion goes catastrophically wrong mid-performance—killing the star onstage. The crowd screams. The curtain drops. And all eyes turn to Ampers.

He had means. He had motive. No one else knew the design. He insists he knows what he built.

Then And appears: a silent, sharp-eyed figure who slides him a notecard. “This illusion has two missing beats.”

Reluctantly, they begin to investigate together—tracing rehearsal footage, backstage rivalries, and long-standing tensions in the act. Ampers starts to spiral: if he could be manipulated, who is he really? And? And just keeps showing up—quietly offering Ampers the one thing he no longer believes he deserves: help. The killer’s identity is revealed—maybe a rival, maybe someone inside the act, maybe even the magician himself, who switched places with a double to escape. The truth wasn’t in the mirrors or the locks. It was in the assumptions everyone made about who was onstage. In the end, Ampers is cleared. But his name is still dirt. And vanishes the same way he arrived—without a word.

A week later, Ampers receives an envelope. Inside: a flyer for a traveling sideshow, and a handwritten note: “Another trick. No explanation.Interested? — &”


### Comps

_Jonathan Creek_ meets _Knives Out_ with the visual grammar of _The Prestige_. 

### Themes

Perception vs. Reality. Misdirection as Weapon. Illusion isn’t just entertainment; it’s survival. Identity and Reputation. Once your name is dirt, does the truth even matter? Cleverness as Armor. When vulnerability feels like a liability, performance becomes a shield. “Suspension of Disbelief” vs. “Falling for Bullshit.”

### Tagline

_Everybody saw the murder. That's what made it the perfect crime._

### Writer's Note

This isn’t a “queer story,” but it is a story about perception, misdirection, and identity. About being misunderstood on purpose because being understood feels dangerous. This is a story about what we show, what we hide, and what it costs to be seen clearly.  

&’s silence is a choice—but for autistic, trans, or neurodivergent viewers, it might not feel like one. The emotional engine is clear: it’s about people who communicate poorly, but connect deeply.

Mostly, it’s about being _clever_ because vulnerable feels _unsafe_.
