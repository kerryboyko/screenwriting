A documentary that unpacks how the very architecture of American governance—not corruption, not partisanship, not even Trump—led to systemic collapse. With humor, data, and bitter clarity, it reframes U.S. history as a design flaw playing out exactly as predicted.

## Synopsis

This isn’t about one bad President. Or one broken election.

It’s about structural incentives—the mathematical systems that define how American democracy works… and how they rewarded destructive behavior. The film traces how America’s collapse was engineered by inertia—and how civics education hid the levers of that collapse from public understanding, without necessarily meaning to.

Exploring topics like: Winner-take-all voting vs. proportional representation. Electoral math and geographic power distortions. Constitutional rigidity and the myth of amendability. Why a campaign contribution provides a stronger incentive than a ballot box. Why “voting harder” can’t fix game-rigged mechanics. 

It will treat political systems as objects of rigorous investigation rather than ideological allegiance. We're not looking for bad actors. We're looking for what happens when everyone follows the rules, and the rules produce terrible outcomes. 

### Comps

_Cosmos_ if it were about _social_ science. _Charlie Brooker_ does _Schoolhouse Rock_. If _An Inconvenient Truth_ was a politician talking about scientific problems, this would be a scientist talking about _political_ problems. 

### Themes

Systems vs. individual. Design as destiny. The failure of reformism. Institutional gaslighting. Structural rot. Why civic myths are deadlier than conspiracies. What happens when everyone follows the rules, and the rules produce terrible outcomes?

### Tagline

_You don't need a conspiracy. Just ordinary people following the rules._

### Writer's Note

This is the kind of project I tried to make 20 years ago—and it failed. Because the math was clear, but the story wasn't. Because the creator was passionate, but completely, at the time, incompetent. Because I thought the message was the content—when in reality, structure is the message.
