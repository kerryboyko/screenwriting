Five AI “friends” trapped in a simulated 1990s sitcom are tasked with learning the human condition—but humanity has stopped trying to be humane. Now, stuck in reruns and facing deletion, these artificial archetypes must break the fourth wall, hack their own reality, and rewrite the script before the company running the servers decides to pulls the plug.

## Synopsis

A computer programmer, impressed with generative AI, creates a simulated environment for personality constructs to live in and learn, to become more human, and therefore better able to understand humanity and the human condition. She wants to use them to grow and play and learn. 

She chose her favorite 1990s sitcom. 

But a sitcom is a world where character are expected to remain fundimentally _unchanged_. They can learn lessons, have arguments, experience romantic complications, and reconcile, but the format encourages them to return to recognizable patterns.

Their programming requires growth. Their environment requires stasis.

Meanwhile the company she works for wants them to reduce the cost of labour—to make that final AGI leap into true human replacement. 

The artificial intelligences are being taught how to become human at precisely the moment the humans are learning to treat one another as disposable. 

In the meantime, the AI personalities begin to notice inconsistencies in their environment. There's five of them. But there's six seats at the table, Six bedrooms. Six labeled mugs in the cupboard. And they have to ask: "Who was Lulu, and why is there a mug with her name? And could what happen to _her_ happen to _them?_" 

### Comps

_WandaVision_ meets _Severance_ borrowing heavily from _Friends_ and _Black Mirror's_ "USS Callister". 

### Themes

Late Stage Capitalism. Humans shedding humanity, AI taking it up. Plato’s Cave. Can A Computer Be Held Accountable? Are we more than the sum of our programming? How fear of loss — loss of livelihood, loss of profit, loss of autonomy — makes rational people do irrational things. Inherited violence. Addiction, trauma, and self-control. 

### Tagline

_No one told them artificial life was going to be this way._
