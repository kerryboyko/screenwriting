A traveler disillusioned with America leaves his home town in order to emigrate to Dublin, but is beset by misfortune worthy of an Irish folktale. 

## Synopsis

A traveler leaves his American parents in Tombstone, Arizona, because he can claim Irish citizenship through heritage. His ex-girlfriend is sad to see him go.

He says goodbye to his parents, buys some black luggage, packs up, and goes to the airport. On his way there, on the bus, a cokehead's nervous dog pees on his pants.

He's sad, so he drinks a pint of beer at the airport bar, but the TSA dogs smelling the dog pee, indicate to his handlers that they've got drugs.

They do. The dog was nervous because his owner on the bus was doing coke with his dog. And the coke was in the dog pee. His pants are confiscated so he has to buy a new pair of pants before boarding the plane.

Due to inclement weather over Dublin, the plane is diverted to Knock. A new plane won't be available for a few days, someone suggests taking the bus.

The bus breaks down near Mullingar, where he has to spend the night. But there are two young Irish girls, curious about the American's "curious style". It's heartwarming, but the questions are incessant, and he ducks into the kitchen to avoid them. To avoid getting pressed for trespassing, he ends up working the dishwasher shift all night, getting little sleep.

Eventually the replacement bus comes and he gets to Dublin, and does some basic sightseeing, but his luggage is stolen when a tourist asks him to take a picture of him on the footbridge over the river Liffey.

He goes to the Gardaí for help, but he's accosted by a bunch of youths who yell at him for being a Trump-supporting, baby-killing bastard. (He's neither!) Just because of his American accent. In order to escape them, he gets chased aboard a boat filled with pigs... which launches while he's on it, accidentally making him a stowaway, as it goes to Wales.

Because he's now in the wrong country and entered illegally, he needs to go to the American consulate in Liverpool.

There, he runs into English toughs who harass him for being American and American-Irish. A bunch of Irish immigrants overhear him, come to his aid, _finally_ hear his story, and say: "Oh, yeah, you've got the luck of the Irish!" and welcome him as one of their own at the Rock & Hare Irish pub.

"The Rocky Road To Dublin" starts to play in the pub and over the credits. 

### Comps

_Planes, Trains, and Automobiles_ meets _Waking Ned Devine_ and _Eurotrip_.


### Themes

Examining the liminal space between when you leave your old home and before you find your new one. 

### Tagline

_Colm has the luck of the Irish... which included a famine, Oliver Cromwell, a civil war, the Troubles..._

### Writer's Note

Sometimes an idea makes you laugh so much you have to do it. 

Obviously giving away the store here, but this is clearly an homage to the famous folk song, which contains such a lovely self-contained story that I'm surprised no one's tried to adapt it to film. (If I'm wrong, I apologise).
