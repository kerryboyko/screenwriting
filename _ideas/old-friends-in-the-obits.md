When far-right figures start turning up dead, a burned-out Miami detective and a nosy true crime podcast crew uncover a chilling pattern: every killer is a woman in her eighties—and none of them feel guilty. As they race to stop the next murder, one question haunts them: should they?

## Synopsis

Across Miami, old women are killing fascists.

Some lost trans grandkids to suicide. Others lost children to a broken healthcare system. One remembers her sister—who seduced and killed Nazis in WWII. Inspired by vigilantes like Luigi Mangioni, they’ve decided their twilight years won’t be spent in quiet despair. They’re taking justice into their own hands.

Their methods? Varied. Their motivations? Crystal clear. Their confessions? Immediate and proud.

They call themselves The Obituary Girls. And none of them will name the others.

The detective isn’t racing to solve a case—she’s scrambling to stop a movement that may already be unstoppable. And when the law no longer serves justice, is enforcing it an act of courage—or complicity?

### Comps

_The Thursday Murder Club_ (if the old people were the killers) meets _The Good Fight_ using the methods of _Dexter_ and _Law Abiding Citizen_

### Themes

Vigilante justice. Moral responsibility during the rise of fascism. The limits of the law. The ethics of resistance. Generational trauma and memory. Responsibility to the future. 

### Tagline

_She can arrest murderers. She can't arrest the idea that inspired them._

### Writer's Note

This isn’t a call to violence—it’s an interrogation of it. A fictional moral inquiry into grief, rage, and what happens when those society deems “harmless” decide they’ve had enough. But yes: it could be misread, especially in a volatile political climate. It walks a tightrope between satire and sincerity—and that’s exactly why it matters.
