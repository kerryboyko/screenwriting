After the Iraq War shatters his faith in American democracy, a burned-out activist sets out to make a documentary on New Zealand’s voting system to spark reform back home—but he’s broke, alone, and about to learn that game theory, like democracy, doesn’t test well with audiences. If he can’t finish the film, he’ll lose his last chance to believe any of it ever mattered.

## Synopsis

At the tail end of the Bush Administration, Ryan—an autistic, fiercely idealistic American—is watching civil rights erode and democracy falter. Determined to stop the decline, he sets out to prove that proportional representation saved New Zealand and could do the same for the U.S.

He takes a sabbatical from his tech job, flies to New Zealand, and ropes in Eileen—a local film student just trying to finish her degree—as his unwilling Sancho Panza. While Ryan dreams of a viral political doc that will shift American policy, Eileen fights to keep the camera batteries charged. 

What starts as an act of civic hope becomes a tragicomedy about overreach, disillusionment, and the strange, beautiful thing that happens when you _fail with your whole chest._

### Comps

_Little Miss Sunshine_ meets _Hamilton_ and _Moneyball_... if no one listened to Billy Beane. 

### Themes

Failed utopias and political burnout. The tension between genius ideas and mediocre execution. Expatriate guilt and savior complexes. The myth of national exceptionalism. What it means to try—and still fail—and be better for it.

### Tagline

_He went to the ends of the world to find the American Dream. He found something better._

### Writer's Note

The story is deeply autobiographical and involves real people—some of whom are still active in politics. It requires sensitivity, legal caution, and emotional distance I’m only just now developing. But the bones are strong, and the emotional truth is ready.
